00 · The mark

The tab, as an icon.

A graphite tile with the chrome ring and a coral arc from 12 o'clock. It is the app icon, the favicon and the nav mark. Use the supplied file, never a redraw.

96 · 48 · 32 · 16 px. Smallest use: 16 px favicon.
tabNav lockup: 30px icon beside a 22px wordmark, 10px gap, Funnel Display 600.
  • Clear space a quarter of the icon's width on every side.
  • Keep the arc coral, starting at 12 o'clock. It means "spent", so it never fills the ring.
  • Don't recolour, outline, rotate, flatten the chrome or add effects.
  • Files favicon.ico, favicon-32, apple-touch-icon (180), icon-192, icon-512, logo-mark.
TAB brand book03
01 · The ring

The ring is the tab.

TAB has one signature object. A chrome ring stands for a tab's limit, and everything it shows is real data. It appears in the hero, on the dashboard dial, on every tab in the list and on the tab page.

3.10USDC left of 5.00
  1. AChrome band

    The whole limit. Always the full circle. Drawn as a metallic conic gradient, never flat.

  2. BCoral arc

    What has been spent, clockwise from 12 o'clock. Coral appears here because spending against a limit is what coral means.

  3. CHeld arc

    Payments signed but not yet confirmed on chain, in translucent coral right after the spent arc.

  4. DThe number

    What is left, in Geist Mono with tabular figures. The line under it reads "USDC left of 5.00", with the limit in coral.

  5. EStates

    Limit reached: the arc fills the ring and the copy says so. Revoked or closed: the arc turns grey and the band dims.

TAB brand book06
04 · Using coral

Coral means stop.

The single accent carries meaning, so it stays rare. If something is coral, the reader should expect a limit, a refusal or an action that can't be undone.

✓ Use for

Limits

The spent arc on the ring, the limit figure after "of", the dial's fill and knob ring, a tab that has reached its limit.

✓ Use for

Refusals

Refused payments and their reasons, failed checks with their error code, form errors, the "TAB can't" heading.

✓ Use for

Destructive actions

"Revoke tab", the revoked status, the "pull the plug" path. Plus the toggle when it is switched on, as the single interactive exception.

✕ Never for

Decoration

No coral headlines, backgrounds, gradients, glows or highlights for emphasis. Emphasis comes from size and the muted second beat.

✕ Never for

Success

A paid payment is paper or soft green, never coral. Coral on a success state would read as an error.

✕ Never with

A second accent

No blue links, no purple, no brand colours from partners. Wallet logos keep their own colours because they are other people's marks.

06 · Voice

Say what happens. Nothing more.

Short, declarative, specific. A headline is a plain statement followed by a muted second beat.

Give your agent a tab. Not your wallet.

Four steps. One signature.

Your money never leaves you.

  • Plain words first. Tab, limit, key, close it out. Save delegate, allowance and token account for the docs.
  • Name the cause and the fix. "Not enough left on this tab for this payment." No apologies.
  • True twice over. "It can't spend past the limit" is enforced by TAB's ledger and by the token program. Claims like that, or none.
  • Numbers are exact. 0.20 USDC, 1,000 USDC beta cap, about 0.002 SOL rent. Never "low fees" or "lightning fast".
  • No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no emoji. Commas and full stops do the work.

08 · Layout & motion

Calm, then exact.

Scroll drives the story and always rewinds. Motion uses transform and opacity only, so it stays smooth on a weak laptop, and parks when it's off screen.

Ease

cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)

Everything: entrances, the ring, panels, receipts printing. Fast start, long soft landing.

Spring

cubic-bezier(.2, 1.35, .4, 1)

Only for things you touch: pill chevrons, switches, the FAQ chevron, the stamp and the key ticket.

Side gutter16–56px

clamp(16px, 4vw, 56px). 16px on phones.

Panel radius20–28px

clamp(1.25rem, 2vw, 1.75rem). Pills fully round. Receipts 6px with a torn edge.

ListsRuled

Hairline rules, numbered only when order is real. No uniform card grids.

Reduced motionStill

All transitions off; the hero shows a still frame with real numbers.

09 · Never

What TAB doesn't do.

These are the common tells of generated, generic product pages. Each one dilutes the single idea the brand rests on.

  • Gradient or rainbow text
  • Glows, neon edges, floating orbs
  • Pulsing "live" dots and green status pills
  • Emoji as icons or section markers
  • Uniform grids of identical cards
  • A second accent colour, or purple
  • Typing effects and fake terminals that loop forever
  • Buzzwords: "revolutionary", "seamless", "lightning fast"
  • Coral used for decoration or for success
  • Stats, logos or testimonials that aren't real
